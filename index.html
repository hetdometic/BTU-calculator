Cooling model

The estimate adds heat through the roof, walls, floor, door and windows; solar gain; outside-air leakage; people; appliances; moisture; and duct loss. Hot-soak recovery is kept separate from the continuous load.

Cooling = conduction + solar + outside air + people + appliances + moisture + duct loss

Heating model

The estimate adds steady heat loss through the RV shell and outside-air leakage. It does not credit people, cooking, or sun, so a cold-night recommendation is not dependent on temporary internal heat.

Heating = envelope loss + outside-air loss + duct loss

Slide geometry and load accounting

For each deployed slide: floor = roof = underside = length × depth; added volume = length × depth × slide height; outer wall = length × height; both end walls = 2 × depth × height. Subtract the length × height attachment opening from the main wall once. Slide windows replace opaque slide wall area and are excluded from main-body windows. The result includes incremental conduction, mixed-sun gain, added-volume infiltration, entered extra seal CFM, latent cooling and the existing duct allowance.

Effective assembly R-values include framing and surface effects. A bridge multiplier above 1 is only for user-estimated nominal-R correction; the default is 1. No universal slide leakage coefficient or extra distribution penalty is assumed. Mixed-sun cooling, inherited construction, unmeasured seals, nonrectangular/retracted geometry and actual HVAC delivery remain validation gaps. This is a planning model using HVAC principles, not an RV-validated Manual J or full hourly heat-balance calculation.

Method, assumptions & sources

How it works

This is a steady-state RV heat-load model, not a square-footage multiplier. It estimates the heat entering during hot weather and leaving during cold weather, then compares the expected load with common equipment capacities.

Step 1

Build the RV geometry

RV type supplies starting dimensions and average main-body window size. Each measured slide adds floor, volume, three walls, roof and underside. Its attachment opening is removed from the main wall; slide glazing replaces slide wall area.

Step 2

Calculate cooling

The model adds shell and window conduction, fixed mixed-sun gain, air leakage, ventilation, people, appliances, humidity and cooling-duct loss.

Step 3

Calculate heating

The model adds heat loss through the shell and windows, cold outside air and heating-duct loss. It does not rely on temporary heat from people, cooking or sun.

Step 4

Suggest a capacity

The expected cooling load is compared with A/C nameplate BTU/hr. Heating is compared with published furnace output, which can be lower than the input number in a model name.

What the basic answers control

  • RV type: starting dimensions and main-body window area per window. Slides are entered individually; none are assumed from RV type.
  • Length, width and height: conditioned floor area, shell area and air volume.
  • Construction: wall, roof and floor R-values; window performance; and air changes per hour (ACH).
  • Slide-outs: measured geometry, separate glazing, effective assembly insulation and optional extra seal leakage. Main-RV ACH applies to the expanded volume. Retracted slides are excluded from deployed additions.
  • Windows and travelers: glass conduction/solar area plus sensible and moisture load from people.

Why the result is a range

Low, expected and high are three planning scenarios—not a statistical confidence interval. The scenarios vary insulation effectiveness, leakage, window transfer, solar/internal gains and duct allowance because those values are usually uncertain in an RV. Air leakage often has the largest effect.

Where the method and equipment data come from

The model uses standard envelope heat-transfer, ventilation/infiltration and psychrometric relationships described in HVAC load-calculation references. The code is intentionally simplified for RV planning and is not ACCA-approved Manual J software.

Reading the result

Sizing guide

  • A/C: first checks the listed FreshJet 5 ≈11K, 13.5K and 15K models individually. If one unit cannot meet the load, it checks all pairs of these models, including two of the same model, up to 30,000 BTU/hr combined nominal cooling.
  • Furnace: compares required heat with published delivered output—not only the larger input number printed in the model class.
  • Highlighted option: is the smallest fitting single A/C, then the smallest fitting pair, or the smallest delivered furnace output at or above the unrounded expected load. Each product keeps its model number, quantity, SKU and product link. A zero load has no equipment recommendation.
  • Two-unit options: add nominal cooling capacities assuming simultaneous operation. They do not establish the load in each zone or confirm roof, controls, airflow and electrical compatibility. An installer must verify the installation. A total containing the ≈11K model remains approximate.
  • Heating: sizing uses verified furnace outputs, up to 30,400 BTU/hr delivered. Heat-pump variants can be browsed, but their cooling BTU ratings are not heating ratings. Heating performance at the outdoor design temperature must be verified before using a heat pump to meet a heating load.
  • Advanced: lets you apply known insulation, window, leakage, weather and duct information. An applied-status label and Clear action remain visible beside the Advanced button.
BTU sizing alone does not confirm roof fit, duct compatibility, electrical or gas service, controls, venting or safe installation. Have the final equipment choice verified by a qualified RV HVAC installer.
